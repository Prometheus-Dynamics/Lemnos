# M41 board check: the reference board's sensors reach `running ok` with no client (untested on board)

Status: **untested on board.** This file states what the second check must show, before it is run. It is the same
procedure as the M37 check (`M37.md`): the three services behind the failsafe timer, our daemon for 120 s, captures at
+15 s and +90 s, the services restored, and a comparison with the baseline.

What changed (rev under test: the M41 commit on `pr/M41`): the BMI088, BMM150 and INA2xx drivers do their bring-up on
the host's ticks, not on a client's reads. The host ticks each device once at bind, and a driver returns the time of
its next step (the chip's own waits: 1 ms, 5 ms, 3 ms, 30 ms). A failed step is retried by the tick from 100 ms,
doubling, capped at 8 s. Reads before a device is ready return `AGAIN` and step nothing. The daemon logs one line per
device when it first reaches `running ok` (`lemnosd: <id>: running ok <ms> ms after bind`).

## The board file

The M37 reduced file, unchanged (`identity`, `imu` bmi088, `mag` bmm150, `power` ina2xx, `cpu-thermal`). Its buses and
addresses match the image's own board file. It changes nothing on the board beyond our daemon's own run: no overlay,
no `config.txt`, no slot, no reboot, no kernel driver.

## Must show (written before the run)

The capture uses `lemnos-ctl list` and `describe` only. No `read` is made before the +15 s capture.

1. **At +15 s, `list`:** `identity`, `imu`, `mag`, `power` and `cpu-thermal` are all `running ok`. The three sensors
   have no reason text. Nothing reads `Status(-N)`.
2. **The daemon's own log, from bind:** one line per device, each `running ok <ms> ms after bind`, for `imu`, `mag`
   and `power`, with `ms` at most 250 for each. (`identity` and `cpu-thermal` are logged too.) A device that never
   reaches `running ok` logs nothing, and the check fails.
3. **At +90 s, `list`:** the same five devices `running ok`.
4. **No client reads at all before the +90 s capture,** so the bring-up is the daemon's own. The run's `lemnosd.out` and
   journal show no `read` from our `lemnos-ctl` before +15 s.
5. **GPIO (item 2 below):** the levels of every line the image's daemon holds, recorded before its stop and after the
   restore, are equal, or the difference is explained.
6. **After the restore:** helios-engine's cameras are served again (its `cameras changed ... served=` line), and
   `lemnos-orion` is `active`. The comparison with the baseline is equal, as in M37.

If any of 1 to 3 fails, the check is NOT CLEAN, and the record says which device, with its reason and its log line.

## The USB power switches (item 2)

The image's daemon holds two GPIO line requests while it runs (`info-baseline.txt` in the M37 record, fds 16 and 17,
`anon_inode:gpio-line`). Its `usb-a-power` and `usb-c-power` are `gpio-power-switch` devices (`channels: [power.on]`,
`controls: [power.on, power.reset]`). They feed the USB ports' power. The captured baseline does not give their GPIO
line names or polarity, so this check cannot say which lines they are from the run's files.

What the driver does (`drivers/gpio-power-switch/src/lib.rs`): at bind it requests the output with the `default_on`
level (default `true`) in one configuration (line 64-66); it never waits. It has no stop action of its own: when the
driver is dropped, the line request is released, and the driver turns nothing off. It restores nothing by itself and
has no auto-off.

**Decision: the check confirms afterwards; it does not hold the lines.** Our reduced board file does not name the two
switches, and the run's files do not give their lines. Naming them blind could drive a pin the board does not expect
(polarity and line are both unknown here). So for this window the check states beforehand that stopping the image's
daemon may drop USB power for the window (a released output keeps its last level in the kernel's gpio chardev, but this
is not verified on the board). It records the levels of the held lines before the stop (`gpioinfo`, or the
`/sys/kernel/debug/gpio` listing where present) and after the restore, and checks afterwards (item 6) that helios-engine's
cameras are served again.

## Board check procedure

Same as M37 (`M37.md`, "Steps, in order"), with these differences: the captures are `list` and `describe` only; the GPIO
levels are recorded before the stop and after the restore; the daemon's bring-up lines are read from its `lemnosd.out`.
Our binaries are built on NVMe (`e-cargo.sh`), and the board run stays on the CM5 (Raze #2) under both locks.

## Drivers with timed bring-up that the rule does not yet cover (for M40)

The every-driver host test is NOT in this PR. Building it needs a simulated bus and a board entry for each built-in
driver, and most of them need hardware the simulator does not model. What this PR has: the rule, in
`docs/writing-a-driver.md` ("Bring-up on the host's ticks"), and the three sensors' test in
`testing/crates/sensor-power-up`.

A static scan (not run against the simulator) of `drivers/*/src/lib.rs` for drivers that do not set `TICKS`, return
`AGAIN`, and name a bring-up state in their source found one candidate:

- `vcm` (`drivers/vcm/src/lib.rs`, health at 181 and the `AGAIN` at 211): it starts when its first move is called
  (`powering up: the next move writes the position`). A caller starts it, so it does not reach `running ok` from bind.

Drivers that are not in this list may still fail the rule: the scan looked for the words only. The M40 test is the
check of the full list.

### Expected for this image: `identity` degraded (transient systemd id)

`identity` (system-identity) reads `running degraded (transient: slot-init did not bind (see machine-id-* in the kernel log))`.
This is expected for this image and is a real fault, per the ruling. Doc reference: `docs/identity.md`, the machine id
paragraph ("An id that does not match (a bind that failed, so the file holds systemd's transient id) is refused the
same way, and the device is `Degraded` with `transient: slot-init did not bind ...`"). Cause on Raze #2: `/etc/machine-id`
holds `13fe474f6a30fd29cc0d8bbf8dbe04d4`, systemd's transient id installed by `board-machine-id.service`
(`/usr/lib/board/machine-id`). The derived id for serial `a317bcbee5226d57` is `dca6dc837fafca90a391f2fde9c20a6e`, and it is
never bound: this image's slot-init predates SLOT-MACHINE-ID (e0da1dc3). It clears when the next image's slot-init binds the id.
Not an M41 failure.
