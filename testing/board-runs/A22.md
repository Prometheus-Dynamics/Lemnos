# A22: USB reset, kernel-driver detach, and USB devices on a Linux board

Status: untested on board. `UsbDevice::reset` and `UsbDevice::detach_kernel_driver` are covered on
the mock, on the simulated board, on the microcontroller host's test port, and on usbfs through
the recording stand-in for the kernel (request numbers and structure layouts pinned, every result
the kernel can give mapped). The names `LinuxBoard` accepts are covered on fixture directories. No
reset, detach, claim or transfer has reached a real USB device through this code, and no test
opens anything under the real `/dev/bus/usb`.

What only a board can show:

- that the kernel answers `USBDEVFS_RESET` with `ENODEV` when the device comes back with other
  descriptors, as `drivers/usb/core/hub.c` reads, and with success when it comes back unchanged;
- that `USBDEVFS_IOCTL` carrying `USBDEVFS_DISCONNECT` unbinds the kernel's video driver and the
  claim after it succeeds, and that the kernel leaves the interface unbound afterwards;
- that lemnosd's user may do both (it needs write access to the device node; a detach also needs
  the node not to have had its privileges dropped).

Before running: take the board lock named in `AGENTS.md`. Builds go on NVMe or SSD. The drivers
are not registered by this PR; a maintainer runs `scripts/generate-builtins.py` where needed.

## 1. A firmware load completes and the device re-enumerates

Supersedes the open check in `testing/board-runs/D13.md` ("Re-enumeration needs a reset this
driver cannot issue"). Steps 3 and 4 there still stand as written.

1. Plug the accelerator in, in its boot-ROM state. Note its port and identity:
   `grep -H . /sys/bus/usb/devices/*/idVendor /sys/bus/usb/devices/*/idProduct`.
2. Give it a board entry with `driver = "coral-loader"`, the `usb` need set to its boot-ROM
   identity as `usb:<vendor>:<product>` (four hex digits each), and
   `config = { firmware = "<absolute path to the firmware image>" }`. Start lemnosd.
3. Expect health `Degraded` with `firmware download in progress: not running yet`, then `Ok`.
4. Expect the device to have left its boot-ROM identity and come back on the same port with its
   runtime identity and a new address: compare `idVendor`, `idProduct` and `devnum` under the same
   `/sys/bus/usb/devices/<port>` with step 1. `dmesg` shows one reset and one new enumeration.
5. With `usbmon` on the bus, the last transfer to the boot-ROM address is the GETSTATUS that
   reads `dfuMANIFEST-WAIT-RESET` (or `dfuIDLE` for a manifestation-tolerant device); nothing is
   sent to that address after the reset.
6. Repeat with the `usb` need given as the port, `usb:<port>` (for example `usb:1-1.4`). Expect
   the same.
7. Record anything else: health `Degraded` with `came back as the device it was` means the reset
   returned success and the device kept its descriptors; health `Failed` with `the USB reset was
   not carried out: retrying the reset` means the kernel refused the reset, and the errno is in
   `strace -e ioctl` on lemnosd. Either is a finding against this PR.
8. A reset the kernel refuses cannot be forced on a healthy board, so the retry of the reset
   (backoff 1, 2, 4, 8, 8 s, never giving up, nothing downloaded again) is covered by the unit
   tests only. If step 7 shows a refused reset, record the spacing of the retries.

## 2. A camera interface is detached and claimed

The camera driver that will use this is not merged, so this check drives the backend directly.

1. Plug in a USB camera the kernel binds its video driver to. Find the interface:
   `ls -l /sys/bus/usb/devices/<port>:1.*/driver` shows the kernel driver on the video control and
   streaming interfaces.
2. From a test binary or the camera driver once it is merged: open the device through
   `LinuxBoard` as `usb:<port>`, call `claim_interface(n)` for the streaming interface and expect
   `Busy`; call `detach_kernel_driver(n)` and expect `Ok`; call `claim_interface(n)` again and
   expect `Ok`.
3. Expect `/sys/bus/usb/devices/<port>:1.<n>/driver` to be gone after the detach, and the
   `/dev/video*` nodes of that camera with it.
4. Call `detach_kernel_driver(n)` once more and expect `Ok` with nothing changed: the interface is
   now held through usbfs, which is a claim and not a kernel driver.
5. Release the interface and drop the device. Expect the interface to stay without a driver: the
   kernel driver is not put back. Record what does bring it back on that board (replugging the
   camera does; note whether anything else does).
6. Detach an interface with nothing bound (repeat step 2's detach after step 5) and expect `Ok`.

## 3. One owner

With one device in the board file under two names (its port and its vendor and product), expect
the second entry's bind to fail with `BUSY` and the first to keep working.
