   stepping throughout. Turn the zone back on with `on`, and the duty comes back to the curve's

   Pass: `pwm1_enable` and `pwm1` equal the step 1 baseline within a second, and the fan's

11. Refusal. Make the fan's `pwm1` unwritable (a bind mount over it, the run's note records it). The

    second, and read `pwm1` at the same moment, for about 36 s.
    Pass: while the fan is in, the duty the controller reports equals `pwm1` (the fan's read-back, the

    The retries come at 1, 2, 4 and then 8 s apart. Once the host binds the fan out (`ctl list` shows
    the fan `failed`), the controller's state read fails and the duty item is absent: the duty is unknown
    while the fan is out, and the health row says so. Remove the mount, and the fan is bound again: the

12. Stop. With the controller running, stop the service with `SIGTERM`.
    Pass: after the process exits, `pwm1`, `pwm1_enable` and `cur_state` equal the step 1 baseline.
13. Kill (documents the gap). Start the controller and `kill -9` the service.

## FAN-RELEASE (pr/FAN-RELEASE): the baseline return, and the board's temperatures

Still untested on board: this PR's sim proof is below, and the board rerun is the list at the end.

**Cause.** `release` (fan/1, method 4) puts the fan at full duty, which is the hwmon-fan safe state (pwm1 255). The controller's
`set_enabled(false)` and its drop (the daemon's SIGTERM) called it, so the fan went to full duty and the baseline the take-over
found (pwm1 179, pwm1_enable 1, cooling state 0) was never kept. The rerun above saw pwm1 255 and state 4 after SIGTERM and after
disable, and that is what the code did.

**Fix.** The ruling (the fan returns to its baseline on SIGTERM and on `set_enabled(false)`) needs two operations, because a
failed write still needs full duty:
- fan/1 gains method 5, `hand_back`, marked `#[added]`: the contract's base hash does not move (`docs/wire.md`, `contract_hashes`
  unchanged). `release` keeps its meaning, the safe state for a failure.
- hwmon-fan records `pwm1` and `pwm1_enable` at take-over, before its first write. `hand_back` writes `pwm1` (in manual mode) and
  then `pwm1_enable`, and keeps the baseline until both have worked. Nothing is written if it never took the fan.
- pwm-channel-fan records no baseline (its channel starts at full duty), so its `hand_back` is full duty.
- fan-control: `set_enabled(false)` and its drop call `hand_back`. A failed write still calls `release` (full duty), so
  check 11's write-failure path is unchanged.

## B14-CURVE: a fan the host binds out, its retries, the baseline, and the board procedure

**Ruling (coordinator, 2026-11-18).** A refused fan write stays `IO`, and the host's rule stands: five `IO`s in a row
bind a device out. The controller survives its fan being bound out: while the fan is out it reports the truth, keeps
retrying on 1, 2, 4 and then 8 s for as long as the fan is out, and takes the fan back when the host binds it again.

**Causes.**

- Check 11 stopped after three refused writes. The host bound the fan out after five `IO`s in a row (the controller
  makes two fan calls per attempt, the write and the release). The fan's calls then answered `NO_DEVICE`, so the fan
  itself never saw the controller's later writes, and the controller reported duty 0 after a failed write.
- Check 12 failed after check 11 in one service start. A bound-out fan's drop leaves full duty on it. The next bind
  recorded that full duty as the baseline, so the stop handed back 1000, not 702.

**What is in the branch.**

- A refused write is `IO` in `hwmon-fan` and in `fake-fan`, as the sysfs write is. The host is unchanged.
- The controller asks for the safe state (`release`) on every refused attempt, and reports duty 1000 while writes are
  refused or the fan is out, never 0.
- `NO_DEVICE` from the fan is a bound-out fan: the controller reports `Degraded` with the reason "the fan is bound out
  by the host: full duty, retrying until it is bound again", and its `retrying` gives the attempt count and the next
  try. It retries on the same schedule, and resumes at the first write that works after the fan is bound again.
- The fan's baseline is recorded per attribute path, so a bind after a drop keeps the first take-over's baseline, and a
  hand-back with no write yet still goes back to it.
- `b14.sh` check 11 holds the fan refused with a marker (`<fan>.refuse`), which also refuses the bind (as a read-only
  bind mount does), so the fan stays out until the marker is gone. It reads the attempts from the health row.

**Fail-before lines** (each run once against the pre-ruling behaviour or a single mutation, then restored):

- Daemon, `a_fan_refusing_writes_is_bound_out_at_full_duty_and_control_resumes_when_it_is_bound_again`, with refusals as
  `AGAIN` (no bind-out): `timed out waiting for: the host binds the fan out`.
- Driver (sim clock), `a_fan_bound_out_is_retried_past_the_cap_on_sim_time_and_resumes_when_bound_again`, with the bound-out
  reason ignored: `left: Degraded("the fan write failed: released to full duty, retrying")`, `right: Degraded("the fan is bound out by the host: full duty, retrying until it is bound again")`.
- Host (sim clock), `a_fan_bound_out_by_the_host_is_retried_past_the_cap_and_resumes_when_bound_again`, with `NO_DEVICE`
  treated as a refusal: the same left and right.
- Earlier in this record (still in the tree): `a_refused_write_is_retried_past_the_cap_on_sim_time_and_recovers` (duty
  0 at t=0), `a_rebind_after_a_drop_keeps_the_baseline_the_first_take_over_recorded` (`"255"` against `"77"`), and the
  stop after a bind-out and rebind, `1000` against `702`.

**Sim.** `testing/fan-control/b14.sh sim`: `b14: 53 passed, 0 failed` (full run, final tree). Check 11 read: Check 11: the host binds the fan out, the health row
reads `failed` with `retrying: attempt N, last reason: the fan is bound out by the host: full duty, retrying until it is
bound again, next try in T ms`, the attempts wait 1, 2, 4 and 8 s and then keep 8 s, the file reads 1000 after the drop,
the duty is never 0, and control resumes on the curve once the marker is gone.

**Board procedure (B14 hand step 11, operator).** Check 11 does not run inside the script on the board. Do it by hand
in the file-phase service start, before the stop check 12 makes:

1. Read `pwm1` (the baseline is already recorded).
2. Bind-mount a read-only copy of `pwm1` over the fan's `pwm1`. Refused writes are then `IO`, as the sim's marker makes them.
3. Sample `ctl list` every 0.25 s for about 36 s. Record each new `attempt N` with its time and the `next try in` value.
   Expected: the fan row goes `failed` after about five refusals (three attempts); the controller's row reads
   `retrying: attempt N, last reason: the fan is bound out by the host ...`; the attempts go 1, 2, 3, 4, ... with gaps of
   1, 2, 4 and then 8 s (each ±500 ms of sampling, and at most one controller step later).
4. Read `pwm1` while the fan is out. **Expected difference:** the drop's full-duty write goes through the same read-only
   bind mount, so it is refused. The fan then stays at its last duty, not at full duty. The report says full duty; on the
   board that is true only if the drop's write lands. Record what `pwm1` reads, and raise it with the coordinator before
   relying on the full-duty report on the board.
5. Remove the bind mount. The fan is bound again within the host's backoff, the controller resumes, and `duty` is back on
   the curve within 40 s. Then the stop hook runs check 12, which expects the baseline.

- A bound-out fan appears in `ctl list` as the controller's row with `retrying: attempt N, last reason: the fan is bound out by the host ...`.
- `hwmon-fan` and `fake-fan` are unchanged in status (refused writes stay `IO`). `fake-fan` gained the `<file>.refuse` marker,

## B14-CURVE (ruling): the duty the controller reports while the fan refuses writes is the fan's own

**Ruling (coordinator, 2026-11-18).** The controller reported duty 1000 while the fan was refused or bound out. That
is true only when the drop's full-duty write lands. On the board, when the fan's writes are refused the drop's write
is refused too (the same `pwm1`), so the fan stays at its last duty while the controller said 1000. The report now
says what is known.

**What the controller reports now.**

- Writes work: the duty item is the commanded duty, as before.
- Writes refused, fan still bound in: the duty item is the fan's read-back, read once at the first refused write of the
  run. Health says either "the safe state (full duty) is applied, and the duty item reads it back" (read-back 1000) or
  "the safe state could not be applied: the duty item is the fan's read-back" (read-back below 1000). The duty is
  never a placeholder.
- Writes refused and the read fails too: the duty item is absent, and health says the fan's duty is unknown.
- Bound out by the host: the host answers every call to the fan with `NO_DEVICE`, reads included, so the duty cannot
  be read. The duty item is absent, `state` fails, and health says the duty is unknown until the fan is bound again.
  Each retry is a read of the fan; the first that works is the rebind. The controller reports the duty it reads back
  (full duty if the drop's write landed, the last duty if the drop was refused), and writes resume at the next step.
- Retries are unchanged: 1, 2, 4 and then 8 s, for as long as the fan is refused or out. Bind-out survival, resume and
  the baseline hand-back are unchanged.

**Why the read is made once, not on each retry.** A call that works resets the host's count of failures in a row, and
the host binds a device out after five. A read on every retry would keep the fan from ever being bound out. The one read
at the first refused write leaves the count to reach five, and the fifth refusal is a write, so the controller's own
release answers `NO_DEVICE` in the same attempt: the controller learns of the bind-out at once.

**Stand-ins.** `fake-fan` now refuses the drop when `<fan>.refuse-drop` is there, as hwmon's drop is refused when the
kernel refuses the writes (hwmon's drop ignores its own refused write, and so does the fake now). `<fan>.unreadable` fails
the duty read. The driver's sim fan (`drivers/fan-control/tests/host.rs`) was a stand-in that read fine while it was
bound out, and released without changing the duty; it now fails the way the host and the kernel do.

**Fail-before lines** (the new report swapped back for the old one: duty fixed at 1000, always present; then restored):

- Driver (sim clock): `case_1_...`: `left: Some(1000)`, `right: Some(400)` (the read-back while refused). `case_2_...`:
  `left: Some(1000)`, `right: Some(600)`. `case_3_...`: `left: Some(State { ..., duty: 1000, ... })`, `right: None`

## B14-CHECKS: the checks' tolerances, the flap, and the last board run (pr/B14-CHECKS, 2026-11-18)

This replaces the check definitions above where they differ, and it is what the next board run uses. Nothing in

### The backoff across the flap (BOARD-4's check 11)

What BOARD-4 showed: the host bound the fan out after five refusals and bound it again about a second later, while
the writes were still refused, because binding a hwmon fan makes no write. So the fan flaps for as long as the
refusal lasts.

The question was whether the controller's backoff carries across those rebinds or starts again at 1 s. **It
carries.** No fix was needed. The controller's attempt count is reset only by a write that works
(`Control::wrote`, `drivers/fan-control/src/law.rs`); a rebind (`Control::rebound`) leaves it alone.

Who retries when, second by second, on the real host with the simulated clock
(`drivers/fan-control/tests/host.rs`,
`a_fan_that_flaps_while_its_writes_are_refused_is_retried_on_a_backoff_that_carries_across_the_rebinds`, 180 s of
refusal):

- The controller's wait sets the pace of the writes: 1, 2, 4 and then 8 s. Each attempt is two fan calls, the duty
  write and the safe-state release.
- The host counts the refusals and binds the fan out at the fifth in a row. That is the fourth attempt's write the
  first time (second 8), and the third attempt's after each rebind. The host answers that attempt's release itself,
  and the fan's drop makes one write, refused too.
- The host binds the fan again one second later. That is its own first wait: the fan had run longer than the host's
  8 s cap, so the host's own count had started over. The bind makes no write.
- Bound out, the controller's attempt is a read. It comes at the controller's 8 s, finds the fan in, and the write
  follows at the next step, one second later. It is refused, and the cycle repeats.
- So a flap is 25 s: duty writes at 8 s, 8 s (bound out at this one), then 9 s. Duty writes came at seconds 1, 2, 4,
  8, 17, 25, 33, 42, 50, 58, 67, 75, 83, 92, 100, 108, 117, 125, 133, 142, 150, 158, 167, 175. Bind-outs at 8, 33,
  58, 83, 108, 133, 158. BOARD-4 saw its two bind-outs 25 s apart (04:24:47 and 04:25:12), which is this cycle.

The counts, and the bound each is held to:

| what | bound | derivation | measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| duty writes, first 60 s | at most 10 | 4 in the first 7 s (the waits of 1, 2 and 4 s), then one per 8 s: 4 + 52 / 8 | 10 |
| duty writes, any later 60 s | at most 8 | one per 8 s, and one more for where the window starts | 7 (seconds 60 to 120), 7 (120 to 180) |
| gap between two duty writes past the cap | at least 8 s | the cap | 8 or 9 s, every one |
| every write of any kind, 180 s | two per attempt | the duty write, then the release or (at a bind-out) the drop | 24 duty, 17 release, 7 drop: 48 |

The same test goes red when the backoff is made to start again at a rebind (the attempt count set to 0 in
`Control::rebound`, tried and not kept): `a duty write came less than 8 s after the one before it, past the cap:
gaps [1, 2, 4, 9, 1, 2, 5, 1, 2, 5, ...]`. So the test would have caught a restart.

Through the daemon, in real time (`service/lemnosd/tests/controller_on_fakes.rs`,
`a_refused_fan_is_bound_again_while_its_writes_are_still_refused_and_the_backoff_goes_on`): the fan is bound out,
bound again while the refusal is in place, and the controller's attempt count goes on and never goes back.

One thing about the host's health for the controller, seen in the test and stated here so it is not a surprise: in
the second the fan is out, the controller's health on the host is `failed`, "a required device failed" (the host's
own, for a device whose need is out). It is `degraded` with the controller's reason for the rest of the flap.

### The stand-ins

- `fake-fan` (`testing/crates/fake-devices`): `<file>.refuse` no longer refuses the bind. A bind makes no write, as
  hwmon-fan's does not, so a refused fake fan is bound again while its writes are refused and flaps as the board's
  does. Before this the marker refused the bind too, the fan stayed out, and the sim did not fail the way the board
  does. `<file>.refuse-bind` is there for a fan whose bind does fail.
- `fake-fan` has a resolution: `levels = 255` keeps a duty as the nearest of 255 levels and reads it back from
  that, with hwmon-fan's rounding, so 300 reads back 302 and 900 reads back 902. The sim boards use it. The
  script's exact-equality check from before this change fails on it, as it did on the board.
- `fake-fan` `read_offset` is a planted fault: a read-back that much off the duty the fan holds.

### The tolerances

Each is derived here and in `testing/fan-control/b14.sh`. None is fitted to a run.

- **The controller's duty against the curve: 1 per mille.** The law interpolates in whole numbers and drops the
  fraction (`Curve::eval`); the script rounds. The two differ by at most 1. Unchanged.
- **The fan's read-back against the commanded duty: half the fan's step.** BOARD-4 failed exact equality at 300
  (read back 302) and 900 (902). The fan's `duty` item now says its step: the largest distance between two duties
  the fan can hold. `pwm1` has 255 levels, so that is `ceil(1000 / 255)` = 4 per mille (`hwmon-fan`, `DUTY_STEP`;
  the item said 1 before, which was the unit and not the fan's resolution). A duty is written as the nearest level
  and read back from it (`pwm = round(duty x 255 / 1000)`, `duty = round(pwm x 1000 / 255)`), so the read-back is
  within half a level of the duty asked for: 1000 / 255 / 2 = 1.96, and both are whole numbers, so 2. The script
  reads the step from `lemnos-ctl show fan` and holds the read-back to `step / 2`. On a fan with step 1 that is 0:
  exact equality.
- **The slew.** Over any two samples, t apart, the duty falls by at most `slew x (t + 1000 ms + 100 ms) / 1000 + 1`.
  The controller steps once a second and each step falls by `slew x (time since its last step)`, so the falls
  between two samples add up to the slew times the time from the last step before the first sample to the last
  step before the second: at most t plus one step. The 100 ms is the script's own read, and the 1 the law's
  whole-number fall. The slew is the board file's `slew_per_s`. This replaces "at most 52 in any one second".
- **Check 3, the heat: 1100 ms.** One controller step to read the zone and one script read. Unchanged. A rise is not
  slew-limited (`drivers/fan-control/src/lib.rs`: "Rising is immediate").
- **Check 11's attempt gaps and count.** Each gap is at least its wait less one turn of the script's sampling loop,
  and at most the wait plus two controller steps and one turn (each wait ends at the first step after it, and a fan
  that is bound again is written at the step after the read that found it). An attempt is first seen at the
  sample after it, so each end of a gap is up to one turn late. The turn is the longest one of the run: measured,
  and printed. The count over the 60 s of refusal is at most `4 + ceil((60 - 7) / 8)` = 11.

The curve, the floor and the slew that the checks expect are now read from the board file the service runs
(`--board-file` on the board), not written in the script.

### The REAL phase: what the real zone can be held to

BOARD-4's REAL check failed. The record put it down to the duty lagging the curve while the zone ramps. The
controller does not lag a rising temperature by its slew: a rise is immediate, and only a fall is slew-limited. What
the samples show is the zone itself. The controller reads the zone once a step; the script's reading is another read
at another time; and two reads of this zone a third of a second apart differed by up to 3.3 C in that run (49.05,
51.80, 50.15 C on three reads in a row). The check compared the duty with the curve at the script's reading, so it
failed on the zone's scatter. Widening the look-back did not help (27 to 37 of 180 samples outside, at 2.5 s to
1.1 s). No bound on that comparison can be derived: it would have to be fitted.

The controller's `demand` is the curve at the controller's own reading, and it comes in the same state read as the
duty. So the REAL phase now checks:

- **r2, unchanged.** The zone rose at least 3 C under the load.
- **r3, the law, exactly, on the controller's own demand.** At every sample the expected duty is the target (the
  demand, or the floor if higher) limited by the slew from the samples before it. The duty is never below the
  target. It is above the target only while it falls. It falls by no more than the slew allows (as above), and by
  no less: while the duty stands above the target it has fallen at least `slew x (t - 1100 ms) / 1000 - 1` over a
  stretch t long. A duty that jumps fails the first; a duty that stays off the curve after the slew has had time to
  catch up fails the second.
- **r4, the demand is on the board file's curve.** The controller reads the same zone as the script, so its reading
  is a temperature the zone can report: on the zone's grid, which the script finds from its own readings (the
  greatest common divisor of their differences: 550 mC on the board). Each demand must be the board file's curve at
  a grid temperature, within 1. Nothing here depends on when the controller read the zone, so the scatter does not
  enter. A zone with no grid coarser than 1 mC makes this NOT MEASURED.
- **r5, the demand follows the zone in time.** The controller's last read was at most one step before the script's
  sample. The demand must lie between the curve at the least of the script's readings of that last step (1100 ms
  back) less S and the curve at the most of them plus S. S is the zone's own scatter in the run: the most two of
  the script's readings one sample apart differed by. It is measured each run and printed, not a constant. This is
  the one bound here that is measured and not derived, and it is stated as that. Its job is to catch a controller
  that does not follow the zone at all.
- **r6, held.** After the load the script keeps sampling until the zone has settled, 60 s at most. Settled: the
  readings of the last 5 s span no more than S. Then the duty must be the target at every sample of those 5 s: the
  slew has caught up, and the duty is on the curve at what the controller read. A zone that has not settled in
  60 s is `NOT MEASURED`, never a pass. A zone that settled with the duty still off its target is a fail. The 60 s
  is a wait, not a tolerance; the longest slew-limited fall at the board's settings is (1000 - 300) / 50 = 14 s.

BOARD-4's REAL samples under these checks, on the PC (`BOARD-4/run-B14/records/lemnos-b14/out-real/samples.tsv`):
grid 550 mC, scatter 3300 mC; r2 pass, r3 pass (the duty is never below its target in 180 samples, and every fall
is 50), r4 pass (every demand is the curve at a grid temperature), r5 pass. r6 cannot be read from that run: its
sampler stopped while the duty was still coming down. The bounds were written first and the samples run after.

`NOT MEASURED` is new. The script counts it apart from PASS and FAIL, and exits 3 when nothing failed and something
was not measured.
