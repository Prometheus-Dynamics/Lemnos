# A25: rebinding on a driver's word, and hotplug hints

Untested on board. Everything below ran only against mocks, the simulated board and fake sysfs
trees. What a board has to show:

Board file: the `uvc` entry of `testing/board-runs/D12.md`, once with `bus = "usb:<port>"` and once
with `bus = "usb:<vendor>:<product>"`.

1. Start lemnosd with the camera unplugged. `lemnos-ctl list` shows the device backing off. Plug the
   camera in: it is running within a second, not at the end of the 8 s wait. Note the time from the
   kernel's `add` line in `dmesg` to the state change.
2. If step 1 took a full backoff step, the device node was not openable when the first event came.
   Record which events arrived (`udevadm monitor -k`) and how long the node took.
3. With the camera idle (never started), unplug it. The device goes to backoff with the reason
   "the device is gone" without anyone calling it. This is the driver's idle read of the descriptor
   failing on the dead usbfs handle: if the device stays running, that read does not fail on this
   kernel and the idle check needs another request.
4. Plug it back in: running again within a second, the kernel's video driver detached again.
5. Start a stream, unplug mid-stream, plug back in, with no client calling the device meanwhile: it
   comes back by itself. (This replaces step 8 of D12, which called `counters` until the host
   dropped the driver.)
6. Run lemnosd where it cannot open the kernel event socket (a seccomp filter or namespace that
   refuses `NETLINK_KOBJECT_UEVENT`, and no inotify) and repeat 1 and 5: everything still
   happens, at the backoff's pace.
7. Flap the port power (`uhubctl`) ten times a second for 10 s with the camera attached:
   lemnosd CPU stays low and the device's attempts go up by no more than 4 a second.
8. A firmware-loaded USB device (`coral-loader`): unplug during the download. The loader goes to
   backoff as "the device is gone", and on replug the download starts over. After a download that
   ends well the loader stays running and healthy although its device left the bus.

9. Names that are not USB (A33). With a board entry on `ttyUSB0` (a USB serial adapter) and one on
   `i2c-N` of an adapter that can be removed (an `i2c-gpio` or `i2c-tiny-usb` overlay, loaded and
   unloaded with `dtoverlay`): start lemnosd with both absent, then add each. Each device's bind
   is tried within a second of the kernel's `add` line, not at the end of its wait. Remove each
   while its driver runs: the driver is ticked at once (a driver that looks at its bus then says
   it is gone). Repeat for a `gpiochipN:LINE` and a `pwmchipN:CHANNEL` behind a removable overlay
   and a `spidevB.C` bound and unbound through `/sys/bus/spi/drivers/spidev/{bind,unbind}`.
