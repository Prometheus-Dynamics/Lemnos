# B11 board run: ina2xx on a CM5

Status: untested on board. The register layer and the driver are covered by unit tests on the
simulated bus (`backends/sim`). No transfer has reached a real INA226, INA238 or INA260.

Setup: one monitor wired to a CM5 I2C bus, with a board file device such as:

```toml
[[devices]]
id = "power0"
driver = "ina2xx"
bus = "i2c-N"
config = { chip = "ina238", address = 0x40, shunt_ohms = 0.010, max_current_a = 2.0 }
```

The INA260 ignores the shunt and current keys. Use the matching entry for the chip fitted.

1. Build the driver for aarch64 and bind the device with the chip present.
   Pass: bind succeeds. Health is `Degraded` until the first read, then `Ok`.
2. Read the device with no load on the rail.
   Pass: `bus_v` matches the supply within 0.5 V, and `current_a` is within 2 mA of zero.
3. Apply a known load, and compare `current_a` and `power_w` with a bench meter.
   Pass: `current_a` is within 2% of the meter reading, and `power_w` is within 2% of volts × amps.
4. Read the calibration register over the bus with `i2cget` (0x02 for INA238, 0x05 for INA226).
   Pass: the INA238 value is 2000 (0x07d0) at 10 mΩ and 2 A. The INA226 value is 2048 (0x0800) at
   10 mΩ and 8.192 A.
5. Disconnect the chip's I2C wiring during reads, then reconnect it.
   Pass: reads fail with an error and health shows `Degraded` while disconnected. After
   reconnecting, readings resume without a restart, and the calibration register is written again.
6. Point the device at an address nothing answers (for example 0x41).
   Pass: bind succeeds and reads fail. The driver keeps accepting reads and does not switch itself
   off. Retry pacing is the host's, and it stays within the 8 s cap.
7. Configure `chip = "ina226"` against an INA238.
   Pass: reads fail as a wrong chip (`NOT_SUPPORTED`), and no calibration write reaches the chip.
8. Time 1000 reads of an idle chip on the CM5, and record the median and worst case here.
   Pass: every read is a single I2C transaction (one `transaction` call per poll), and the timings
   are recorded. CM5 timings only.
