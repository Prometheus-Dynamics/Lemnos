# A31: a buffer given back and named again while jobs are read

Status: untested on board. The change is in how the job-serving drivers and the ISP drivers
collect the buffers a consumer gave back: before each job they read, not once a round. It is
covered per driver against played-back hardware, with the consumer acting in the middle of a
round. No test goes near a real block.

What only a board can show: a consumer on another core that takes a completion, gives its
buffer back and at once posts a job naming it, for long enough that the driver reads such a job
in the round the buffer came back in. On any board with one of these blocks:

1. Bind the driver and attach a consumer written on the client (`engine/client`,
   `Session::open_stream_writable`) that uses two buffers only, so every job's output is the
   buffer of the completion just taken, with no pause between taking a completion and posting
   the next job. No such tool is in the repository yet; it has to be written for the run.
2. Let it run for a million jobs. Every job posted must complete: the consumer's count of
   completions equals its count of jobs, and it never waits longer than one job takes.
3. Read the driver's counters: `errors` (image and inference blocks) or `dropped` (ISPs) stays
   0, and the stream's `faults` stays 0.
