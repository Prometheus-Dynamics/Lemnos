# B9: bmm150

Status: untested on board. The register map, trim compensation, power-up sequence and the
retry-on-fault path are covered by the simulated-bus tests in `drivers/bmm150`. No transfer has
reached a BMM150.

## Setup

On the CM5, with a BMM150 wired to an I2C bus that the Linux I2C backend (B2) exposes,
and a board file that names the bus as `i2c-N` and the magnetometer as the `bmm150` driver. Take the
board lock from `AGENTS.md` before starting. Build on NVMe or SSD, never on the Github HDD. Report
CM5 timings only.

## Checks

1. Bind the driver at the chip's address (0x10 with SDO and CSB2 low) and read it twice in a row.
   Pass: a read that lands before the chip's start-up tick returns `AGAIN`, and the second read returns three
   finite values in tesla. The host ticks the driver from bind: the first tick powers the chip on and the tick
   3 ms later starts it (see the header of `drivers/bmm150/src/lib.rs`). A first read after that returns values,
   and that is a pass too. Record the time from bind to each read.

2. Hold the sensor still and read it 20 times over two seconds.
   Pass: all three components are finite, the magnitude is within a few percent of the value the site's
   reference gives (state the reference), and the per-axis standard deviation over the 20 reads is recorded
   against the preset's documented noise. A consecutive step of more than 1 µT is not a failure at the
   `regular` preset: its documented 1-sigma noise is about 0.6 µT per axis, so a vector step of about 1.5 µT
   rms is expected. `high_accuracy` (0.3 µT rms) is the preset that can hold a 1 µT step bound.

3. Turn the sensor through a full turn in a horizontal plane while reading it.
   Pass: the horizontal components (x and y) keep a steady magnitude while their direction turns
   through a full circle.

4. Bring a magnet within a few centimetres of the chip, then take it away.
   Pass: the field changes by more than 100 µT while the magnet is close, and returns to the
   still-reading value once it is removed.

5. Set `preset = "high_accuracy"` and `data_rate = 2`, then read every half second.
   Pass: readings change at most every 500 ms, and a read that comes before a new conversion repeats
   the previous value.

6. Set `address` to 0x11 while the chip is at 0x10, then read.
   Pass: the bind is refused as absent (`NO_DEVICE`, reason "no device at 0x11: nothing answers at its
   address"). Nothing is bound, so the host has no driver instance: reads of the device find no items and
   return `NO_DEVICE`, and health reads "retrying (attempt N, bind failed: ...)". The host backs off with the
   retry delay capped at 8 s, and the device is not switched off. A read that fails on a bound driver is check 7.

7. With the correct address, disconnect the sensor's power for five seconds while reading, then
   reconnect it.
   Pass: reads fail while the sensor is absent, and after it returns the next read gives `AGAIN`
   and then valid values, with no restart of `lemnosd`.

8. Call read in a tight loop with no pause after the `AGAIN` from check 1, for 100 calls.
   Pass: any failure in the loop is followed by a valid read once the retry succeeds, and the
   process does not panic. Record the failure count and the read timings from the CM5.

## Run on Raze #2, 2026-11-18 (BR-OPEN)

Run on the CM5 (Raze #2, serial a317bcbee5226d57, boot 0f80a3d6-6aa1-4814-ba3d-800bfff5dd06, kernel 7.2.9-v8-16k),
origin/next 25c49ab5, lemnosd and lemnos-ctl for aarch64 musl. The chip is the image's own magnetometer at i2c-1 0x10. Both
board locks were held for the run. The image's lemnos units were stopped behind a failsafe timer and restored; the timer was
cancelled. The first attempt aborted in check 8 (the board's `date` has no `%N`); no reading from it is used. The hypothesis (written before the run)
was written before the run; the raw output stays off the repository.

- **1 Bind and read.** Pass for the second read: three finite values (x 63.4, y 8.2, z 20.5 uT). **Fail for the first read:**
  it returned values, not AGAIN. The daemon finished bring-up 7 ms after bind, before the first client read.
- **2 Still, 20 reads over 2 s.** **Fail.** Magnitude 65.1 to 67.6 uT (mean 66.5), above the record's 25 to 65 uT. Consecutive
  steps up to 3.5 uT as a vector; only 4 of 19 steps had every axis within 1 uT. The record's magnitude range may not suit
  this site; the reading is recorded as it was.
- **3 Turn through a full turn.** Not run: a hands step.
- **4 Magnet.** Not run: a hands step.
- **5 high_accuracy, data_rate 2.** Consistent at 0.5 s polling: consecutive readings repeat in some intervals (63.0, 63.0,
  63.375, 63.375), change in others. Polling at 0.5 s cannot show the update instant, so the "changes at most every 500 ms"
  clause is not decided here.
- **6 Address 0x11.** **Fail on the error reporting.** Every read returned no values with exit 0; the named item read returned
  `no item "magnetic_field.x"`, exit 1. Health: `retrying (attempt 3, bind failed: no device at 0x11: nothing answers at its
  address, next try in 1982 ms)`. The device stayed bound, not switched off (pass for that clause). No IO status was shown, and
  the record's `Failed("bus error")` text did not appear. The 8 s cap was not reached in five reads.
- **7 Power removed.** Not run: a hands step.
- **8 100 tight reads.** Pass: 100 of 100 reads returned values, each a client process. Median 4.7 ms, p99 5.7 ms, worst 6.1 ms.

Restore: lemnos-orion and lemnosd active, `systemctl --failed` 0, helios-engine active, the failsafe timer cancelled, the staged
files and the board's `/var/tmp` records removed. The usb1 profile was in memory only and is deleted.

## B9-FIX: verdicts on the Raze #2 run (2026-11-18), from the code and the chip's documented behaviour

These are driver-side reviews of the record above. They change the record's expectations, not the run's results.

- **Item 1, first read returned values: record wrong.** The driver's contract (`lib.rs` header) is that a read
  is `AGAIN` until the start-up tick, which is 3 ms after the power-on tick the host runs from bind. The daemon
  finished bring-up 7 ms after bind, before the first client read, so the read correctly returned values. The
  record assumed a read always lands before bring-up. Check 1 is corrected above. The mock test
  `first_read_is_again_until_the_start_up_tick_then_reads_values` pins the contract.
- **Item 2, magnitude 65.1 to 67.6 µT and 3.5 µT steps: record wrong for this site; driver maths checked.**
  The compensation matches the Bosch reference form: the integer X/Y and Z formulas and constants agree with
  the float form in the earlier driver (dev's `compensate_xy`, `compensate_z`), the 13-bit and 15-bit fields
  sign-extend by an arithmetic shift, the overflow values are -4096 and -16384, rhall is 14 bits, and the
  result is in 1/16 µT divided down to tesla. The trim register map (0x5D, 0x62, 0x68 blocks, with XY2 at 0x70
  and XY1 at 0x71) is the one the driver cites. The configured preset is `regular` (the default), which sets
  nXY = 9 and nZ = 15 (REP_XY 0x04, REP_Z 0x0e), the documented repetitions. Its documented noise is about
  0.6 µT rms per axis (Bosch community reply; the datasheet table was not fetched). The reading's horizontal
  component is about 63 µT (x 63.4, y 8.2), and Earth's horizontal field is about 40 µT at most anywhere, so the
  field at the sensor has a local source (a magnet, steel or a current loop near the chip). The 25 to 65 µT band
  assumes Earth's field alone. The 1 µT step bound is tighter than `regular` noise, so it is not a pass criterion
  there. Nothing here is a driver change.
- **Item 3, no read status while retrying, and the IO expectation: record wrong; the driver is consistent.** At bind
  the address is probed once. A chip that does not acknowledge is refused as `NO_DEVICE` (absent) and the host
  retries the bind. The driver never exists, so the host answers reads. `read <device>` lists no items for a
  device that is not running and exits 0 with no values, and `read <device> magnetic_field.x` reports `no item`.
  That behaviour is the host's and the control tool's, shared by every driver on next that refuses at bind
  (bmi088, ina2xx), and it is a follow-up for a shared-interface PR, not a driver change. A bound driver whose bus
  fails returns `IO` with health "the chip did not answer on its bus, retrying", which is check 7's case and the
  same as bmi088's read during its retry. The text "bus error" is generic-sensor's, not this driver's.

## Rerun on Raze #2, 2026-11-18 (B9-FIX, origin/next 72c4274a)
