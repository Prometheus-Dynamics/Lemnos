## Stamp model change (pr/BMI-STAMP, after the run above)

This note changes what the checks above measure for the accelerometer, and says what is still open on the gyroscope.

- Accelerometer stamps (checks 3, 4 and 7's accelerometer side) now come from the chip's sensor time
  (`drivers/bmi088/src/sensor_clock.rs`). Each tick's drain reads the sensor time just before and just after the FIFO's
  length, the second time between two host clock readings, and the FIFO's data after both. The two reads bound the
  sample grid's phase; the next drain is aimed at the middle of the bounds, so they close in a few drains. A
  least-squares line through the second reads maps sensor ticks to host time. Before this change the newest sample
  took the tick's time, which carried the tick's host jitter into every batch. The run above (46 gaps over 7.5 ms,
  largest 9.13 ms; span +668 ms) was taken on the tick-time stamps. Repeat check 3 and check 4 on the new build. Pass:
  stamp span within 1 ms of the elapsed time, no gap over 7.5 ms, and no batch miss in health. The model converges
  after 16 host-timed batches with the phase's bounds within 1 ms, about 6 s after bind at 200 Hz (a tick drains about
  every 365 ms). Until then no record is delivered; the first records are the ring's newest 146 (0.73 s) before the
  convergence, and the samples older than those had no time and are dropped without a loss in health. Start the 45 s
  capture at least 7 s after bind (the run above settled 3 s), or count check 3 from the first record delivered.
  `drivers/bmi088/src/clock_tests.rs` runs this capture on the simulated chip with the run's rate (9206 records in 45 s,
  2.29 % fast) and its tick jitter, 9 ms outliers included; every rate from 4 % slow to 4 % fast is within 0.23 ms
  there. The span and gap checks do not see a constant offset of every stamp: on the board the phase's error is
  bounded by the time between the two sensor time reads (one 2-byte register read, about 0.1 ms at 400 kHz), which
  the run cannot measure directly. Record the bus speed.
- Gyroscope rows (check 3's gyro capture, check 4's gyro gaps, check 7's gyro side): the gyroscope has no sensor
  time, so its stamps still come from the tick's time, as in the run above. Its stamp gaps and span are expected to
  show the same tick jitter. The gyroscope's own clock stamping is a follow-up, and check 7 stays open until both
  dies are stamped from clocks.

## Stamping from the first batch (pr/GYRO-STAMP, 2026-11-18)

This note replaces the rules above where they differ. Nothing here is run on the board: `untested on board`.

- Data comes from the first batch. A batch no longer waits for a die's clock to converge. The first samples
  are stamped from tick time (the tick's host time, less the sample periods back from the newest), and the
  model takes over when it converges: about 6 s after bind for the accelerometer (14 batches at 200 Hz on the
  simulated chip), about 16 s for the gyroscope (62 to 70 batches). Until then a stamp can be up to about 9 ms off
  (the tick's own jitter, 8.3 ms worst on the simulated board run). After the switch the model's stamps are within
  1 ms of the true time (0.13 ms and 0.31 ms on the simulated runs).
- How to read the mode: `at_ns` is always a plain time, with no flag bits. A batch read through
  `read_batch` gives the plain stamps, and a reader that uses only `at_ns` gets monotonic, plausible times in
  every mode. The mode is carried beside each record by `read_batch_stamped` (method 3 of `accelerometer/1` and
  `gyroscope/1`, an addition): one byte before each record, 1 for tick time and 0 for the model, so a record is
  29 bytes (`STAMPED_RECORD_LEN`). `read_batch` and its records are unchanged. Health also names tick time while
  a die is in it. The contract hashes are unchanged, since the method is appended and additions leave the base
  hash alone: `0xfa7617318969b33f` accelerometer and `0x6af97be3acc54eb3` gyroscope, before and after.
- Re-anchor: a chip reset, a jump, a FIFO overflow or a failed transfer sends the die back to tick time until its
  model has converged again. Health names the restart.
- Wide brackets: a bracket wider than 1 ms gives its drain no host time. The model takes nothing from that drain,
  and its samples are stamped from the tick. The 1 ms is the phase's own budget (`PHASE_WIDTH_NS`).
- Monotone: a record is never earlier than the last record a batch wrote, across the switch and across a
  re-anchor.
- Visible wide brackets: on the simulated gyroscope, each reading off by up to 9 ms on its own (the brackets are
  visibly wide), 12 runs of 45 s: worst 4.78 ms, largest gap 6.0 ms, against 4.8 ms and 9.7 ms for tick time alone.
  Equal error on both readings of a bracket (which no bracket shows) is not caught: the same runs gave 14.9 ms and
  24.6 ms, and the driver cannot tell that error from a true reading.
- What B8's capture needs now: it no longer has to wait for convergence to see data. Its stamp checks (the span
  within 1 ms, the gaps within 7.5 ms, no batch miss) apply to the model-mode records only. Count the tick-time
  records separately and record how many there were, and when the switch happened. `lemnos-bmi088-batch` reads
  `read_batch_stamped` (`--contract accel` or `gyro`) and prints `b8.tick_records`, `b8.model_records`,
  `b8.mode_changes` (the batch at which each stamping mode began) and `b8.backwards`. Check 3 and check 4 are the
  same checks on model-mode records; check 7 is unchanged and still open.

## Check definitions (B8-CHECK, pr/B8-CHECK, 2026-11-18)

This replaces check 3's span test, and check 7 is now measured by the tool. The tool is
`testing/crates/bmi088-batch` (`lemnos-bmi088-batch`), the rehearsal is `testing/crates/bmi088-batch/rehearsal.sh`,
and the simulated dies are `fake-fifo-accelerometer` and `fake-fifo-gyroscope` in `testing/crates/fake-devices`.
Nothing here is run on the board: `untested on board`.

**Why the span check was wrong.** It compared the span from the first record's stamp to the last record's stamp
with the tool's wall-clock window. The window opens before the first read and closes after the last. The first
record is whatever the first read that returned records fetched, and the last is what the last such read fetched,
so each end can differ from the window by up to one poll period (50 ms here), whatever the stamps are. BOARD-3's
-44.5 ms is one such end, and no stamp error is in it. The 1 ms bound was not derived from anything the stamps
can be held to.

**Symbols.** P is the sample period (5 ms at 200 Hz). For each batch read the tool takes `start` and `end` on
`CLOCK_MONOTONIC`, the clock the stamps are on, before and after the read; L = end - start is that read's latency,
measured, not assumed. TOL = 1 ms is the driver's phase budget (`PHASE_WIDTH_NS`, `drivers/bmi088/src/sensor_clock.rs`):
the model knows the sample phase to within that much.

**(a) Per batch, either mode.** The newest record of a batch was taken before the FIFO was read, and no more
than one period before that read. Its stamp s therefore lies in `[start - P - TOL, end + TOL]`, i.e. the lag
`end - s` lies in `[-TOL, L + P + TOL]`. A negative lag is a stamp after its read. A lag over `L + P + TOL` is a
stamp the chip could not have been holding. Tick-time stamps are held to the same bound: a stamp taken at an
earlier tick is exactly the lag this catches.

**(b) Over the run, model records.** Each model batch's newest record is one point: its record number n, its
stamp s, and its read's end R. Least-squares slopes of s and of R against n give the stamps' rate and the host's
rate for the same records; the rate error is their relative difference in ppm. Each point is compared through
its own read, so the poll period at either end does not enter. The bound: over the points, `R - s` lies in a
band of width W = max over points of `L + P + 2 TOL`. A line fit moves by at most `(W/2) * sum|n - mean| /
sum(n - mean)^2` per record for offsets confined to such a band, so the bound in ppm is that over the host's
slope. For a 45 s, 200 Hz run that is about 300 to 400 ppm (`b8.b.bound_ppm`), so a 1.5% rate error is 40 times
the bound.

**(c) Over every record.** No stamp earlier than the one before it, across batches too, and no gap over
1.5 P (7.5 ms at 200 Hz). Unchanged in substance.

**Verdict.** The capture passes when (a) has no violation and at least one batch, (b) has at least two model
batches and |rate| is within its bound, and (c) has no backwards stamp and no miss, with records delivered. The
tool exits 0 on a pass, 5 when a check fails, and 4 on a data fault (a partial batch, an undecodable record, a
failed read). A failed check is printed as `fail`.

**Start time.** Start the capture at least 8 s after bind, so the model has converged (14 batches at 200 Hz, about
6 s). Check (a) judges every batch, so a tick-time batch in the capture counts: `b8.tick_records` must read 0 for
a pass.

**Check 7 (gyroscope run).** `lemnos-bmi088-batch --contract gyro --device imu --accel-device imu` reads both dies
on one poll. It prints the spread of each gyroscope model stamp's offset to its nearest accelerometer model stamp
(`b8.check7.spread_ms`), and the dies' stamp rates as a ratio in ppm (`b8.check7.rate_ppm`). The spread is at most
about one accelerometer period, by construction (the offset is to the nearest record), so it measures the phase
between the dies, not a drift; the rate is the drift. Both are recorded and not judged. Check 7 stays open until a
clock-based alignment is in `next`.

**What a board run must record.** Every `b8.*` row from the accelerometer capture, with its verdicts `b8.a.verdict`,
`b8.b.verdict`, `b8.c.verdict` and `b8.verdict`; the exit code. The same from the gyroscope capture, with
`b8.check7.*` and `b8.accel.verdict`. The bus speed (the PIO bus at 400 kHz SCL, the default). The time of bind and

## Check (d): the span against the wall window (no read times needed)

The stamps' first-to-last span is compared with the run's wall window (`elapsed`). The bound is derived from how a
stamp is taken relative to the run:

- The first stamp is taken on the first read after the run starts, so at most one poll period (the tool's
  `--period-ms`) after the start of the window.
- The last stamp is taken on the last read before the run ends, so at most one poll period before the end.
- Each stamp is taken to within one sample period (`--sample-period-ms`, 5 ms at 200 Hz).

So the span can differ from the wall window by at most `2 x poll + 1 x sample`: 2 x 50 ms + 5 ms = **105 ms**.
Check (d) passes when `|span - elapsed| <= 2 x poll + sample`. A 1 ms bound, as the old span row had, fails any run;
105 ms is the bound the sampling itself allows.

In replay, `elapsed` comes from `--elapsed-s` and the poll period from `--poll-ms`, because the capture carries
neither. Without both, check (d) is `not_evaluated` and the replay is not a pass. Live, the tool uses its own
measured wall time and `--period-ms`.

## Scoped verdicts, check 7 and the accelerometer's convergence (pr/B8-SCOPE, 2026-11-18)

This replaces the check definitions above where they differ, and it is what the next board run uses. Nothing in
this section was run on the board: `untested on board`. The BOARD-4 captures are judged again here by replay on
the PC, which is a reading of files, not a board run.

Why: on BOARD-4 both dies passed every check once the model had converged, and both captures from bind failed.
Every violation was in a tick-time batch. The tool held those batches to the model's bounds, and a tick-time stamp
is the host's tick time, which the driver documents as up to about 9 ms off. So the from-bind verdict said nothing
about the stamps: it failed because the capture began before the model did.

### The verdicts, per mode

P is the sample period (5 ms). L is the read's own latency, measured for each batch. TOL is 1 ms, the driver's phase
budget (`PHASE_WIDTH_NS`). D is the driver's tick drain period.

Each record carries its mode. A batch is judged on its newest record, by that record's mode.

- **(a), model batches.** Unchanged: `read end - stamp` lies in `[-TOL, L + P + TOL]`.
- **(a), tick-time batches.** `read end - stamp` lies in `[-TOL, L + D]`, with `D = (FIFO depth / 2 + 1) x P`.
  Derivation, from the driver (`drivers/bmi088/src/lib.rs`): a tick-time stamp is the host time of the tick drain
  that stamped the record (`stamp_new`: the tick's time, less whole periods back from the newest sample). A read
  returns what the most recent tick drain stamped. The tick drains every half FIFO depth (`Die::tick`:
  `RING x period / 2`), and each drain is placed on the sample grid by the aim or the spread, both under one period.
  So two tick drains are at most `depth / 2 + 1` periods apart, and the newest tick-time stamp in a batch is at
  most that old when the read starts. The FIFO depths are 146 frames (accelerometer) and 100 (gyroscope), so D is
  **370 ms** and **255 ms** at 200 Hz. The lower end is the model's: a tick-time record after a model record is held
  to the model's last stamp. A host that ticks later than a whole drain period shows as a violation, and that is a
  fault to see.
- **(a), a batch that filled the buffer.** A read takes 4 KiB, 141 records. A batch of 141 filled it, and the driver
  keeps the rest for the next read (`Die::deliver`), so that batch's newest record is not the newest the driver
  held, and neither bound above applies to it. It is counted (`b8.a.full_batches`) and left out of (a) and (b). It
  happens when a capture begins on a backlog. BOARD-4 had none (its largest batch was 82). Found in rehearsal: a
  from-bind case begun 1.3 s after its bind, on a loaded PC, returned 141 tick-time records in its first read, the
  newest 0.58 s old.
- **(b).** Unchanged, and it is the model's: the rate of the model's stamps against the host's. A capture with
  fewer than two model batches has no rate, and fails: a capture passes only if the model published in it.
- **(c), order.** No stamp earlier than the one before it. This is over every record in every mode, and across the
  switch from tick time to the model.
- **(c), gaps.** No gap over 1.5 P between two consecutive model records. A gap with a tick-time record on either
  side is counted and shown (`b8.c.tick.gaps_over`, `b8.c.tick.largest_gap_ms`) and not judged. Reason: a tick-time
  stamp is a tick's time less whole nominal periods, so every such gap falls between two tick drains and carries
  the tick's own timing and the chip's rate error over a whole drain. A lost sample does not show in it. Health
  reports a loss in either mode.
- **(d).** The bound is unchanged: the span of the stamps against the wall window, within `2 x poll + P` (105 ms).
  What the span covers is corrected. The first read of a capture returns what the driver's ring held before the
  capture began: up to the ring's depth, 146 records or 0.73 s for the accelerometer, when nothing read the die
  before. Those stamps are older than the window by as much, and they were in the span. Now a record stamped more
  than one period before the capture's first read is a backlog: it is counted (`b8.d.backlog_records`) and the span
  starts after it. The capture's first read is the first read that returned records, less one poll period for each
  read before it (in tick time the reads before the first tick drain return nothing), which the capture file's new
  `poll` column carries. A file without that column has every record in its span, as before. Found in rehearsal: a
  from-bind capture in the simulator begun 0.17 s after its bind read +121.5 ms and failed (d) on the backlog.
- **The capture.** `b8.model.verdict` is the model's records by the model's bounds ((a), (b), and the gaps of (c)).
  `b8.tick.verdict` is the tick-time batches by theirs ((a)); it reads `none` when the capture has no tick-time
  record. `b8.verdict` passes only when both pass, no stamp goes back anywhere, and (d) holds. A capture with both
  modes is judged per mode. The exit codes are as before: 0 pass, 5 a check failed, 4 a data fault, 3 not measured.

The verdict rows now read `key`, `pass` or `fail`, an empty value and a note, live and in replay (the live tool
used to print `measured` with the verdict as the value). The live rows and a replay of the capture file are the
same text.

### Check 7: the two dies against each other

A gyroscope run reads both dies in each poll. Three things are measured.

- **The spread.** Each gyroscope model stamp is compared with the nearest accelerometer model stamp. Two 200 Hz
  sample grids that are not locked to each other put that offset anywhere within half a period either way, so its
  spread (max minus min) is up to one accelerometer period, plus each die's stamp error (TOL each). The period is
  the accelerometer's own stamp spacing in the run. Bound: **accelerometer period + 2 ms**, 6.96 ms on the board's
  die. What it can show is small. The offset is to the nearest stamp, so the spread can pass one period only where
  the accelerometer's stamps have a hole. It cannot see a constant offset between the dies, and it cannot see a
  drift. Gyroscope stamps outside the accelerometer's span are left out (the tool used to compare them with the
  accelerometer's last stamp).
- **The same poll.** Check (a) holds each die's newest stamp to its own read: within `[start - P - TOL, end + TOL]`.
  So `gyroscope newest - accelerometer newest`, from the two reads of one poll, lies within
  `[g.start - a.end - P - 2 TOL, g.end - a.start + P + 2 TOL]`: the two reads' span, one period, and 1 ms for each
  die, about 7 to 8 ms either way. This is the part that sees a constant offset between the dies.
- **The rate ratio.** The gyroscope's stamp slope over the accelerometer's. The dies have separate oscillators, so
  this is their rates' ratio. Neither the driver nor its notes cite an oscillator tolerance from the chip's
  datasheet, so there is no documented tolerance to hold it to. The only stated range is the one the driver's
  stamping is tested over, 4 % slow to 4 % fast (`drivers/bmi088/src/clock_tests.rs`, the two rate sweeps). Two
  dies inside it differ by at most `1.04 / 0.96 - 1`: **83 333 ppm**. A ratio beyond that means a die is outside
  the range the model is tested for. This is a loose bound and it is stated as one. The tight statement about each
  die's rate is its own check (b).

Check 7 passes when all three hold (`b8.check7.verdict`), and it counts in the run's exit code.

Where BOARD-4's numbers land, having derived the bounds first: spread 4.965 ms and 4.962 ms against 6.96 ms;
offsets -2.485 to +2.481 ms; ratio +7350 and +7320 ppm against 83 333 ppm. The same-poll part cannot be judged from
BOARD-4: its files hold the gyroscope's records only. The next run writes both (`--accel-out`).

### The accelerometer's convergence: batch 44, not 14

BOARD-4's accelerometer published its model at batch 44 (16 s); the notes said about batch 14 (6 s). The cause is
found in the code and the capture, and it is fixed in the driver. No bound was widened.

What the driver needs to publish (`SensorClock::converged`): 16 host-timed batches, and the phase's bounds within
1 ms. A drain whose bracket is wider than 1 ms has no host time and is not counted.

- **Not rejected brackets.** After the model published, a drain with a wide bracket is stamped from the tick, so it
  shows in a capture as a return to tick time. BOARD-4 has none: 0 returns in 79 drains of accel C1 after batch 44
  and 0 in 123 drains of accel C2 (`b8.tick_returns`). The bus is the same before and after, so the brackets were
  being taken, and 16 of them were in hand by about batch 16.
- **The phase's bounds.** That leaves the 1 ms width. The capture shows why it was not met. Each tick drain's time
  can be placed on the model's own sample grid by carrying the model's records back at their rate
  (`b8.tick.grid_offset_ms`, `b8.tick.grid_step_ms`). On accel C1 the drains started at the same point of the grid
  again and again: the median change from one drain to the next is 0.024 ms, against a 4.96 ms period. The
  exceptions are the three drains the host ran late (drains 11, 16 and 28, by 1.4 to 7.7 ms). The gyroscope's
  drains, which are spread on purpose, moved 1.8 ms per drain.
- **Why that stalls.** The bounds are made by the drain's three reads: the sensor time, the FIFO's length, the
  sensor time again. A sample made before the length read lowers the upper bound to the second sensor read; one made
  after it raises the lower bound to the first. The driver aimed the *tick's* time at the middle of the bounds. The
  length read then fell a fixed time after the middle on every drain, so the sample was on the same side of it
  every time, each drain repeated the last one's bound, and the bounds stopped at about twice the time from the
  tick to the second sensor read. On the simulated chip that time was 0.3 ms (bounds 0.6 ms: published at batch
  14). On the board's bus it is longer, the bounds stopped above 1 ms, and the model published only when the slow
  creep of the aim (about 0.015 ms per drain in the capture) or a late tick moved a drain across the sample.
- **Shown on the simulated chip** (`drivers/bmi088/src/clock_tests.rs`,
  `the_accelerometers_model_publishes_on_time_on_a_slower_bus_with_a_quiet_host`): with the three reads 0.2 ms apart
  or more and a host that ticks within 0.02 ms, the driver before this change did not publish in 150 batches (55 s)
  for most phases of the grid, with every bracket taken. So the board's 44 was luck, and a quieter host would have
  stayed in tick time.
- **The fix.** The drain is now due early by its lead, so that the length read is what falls at the middle of the
  bounds. The lead is measured by each host-timed drain: the host time of the second sensor read, less the tick's
  time, less one bracket's width (the length read is one transfer before the second sensor read). The bounds then
  halve each drain, down to the time between the two sensor reads (two transfers, about 0.3 ms at 400 kHz). With
  the fix the same test publishes at batch 14 for every phase at 0.15, 0.2, 0.25 and 0.3 ms between reads. The
  1 ms budget, the 16 batches and the 1 ms bracket limit are unchanged.
- **What the notes now say** (`drivers/bmi088/src/lib.rs`, `sensor_clock.rs`): the model publishes at the first
  drain at which 16 host-timed batches have been taken and the bounds are within 1 ms. With every bracket taken that
  is the 16th drain, about 6 s. Each drain without a host time adds one drain period (365 ms), so a bus where a
  fraction of the brackets is rejected takes proportionally longer, and `b8.tick_returns` counts the rejected ones
  once the model runs. On a bus where the two sensor reads are over 1 ms apart (about 100 kHz) the bounds cannot
  reach 1 ms and the stamps stay tick time. The earlier note that the bounds close to "about 0.1 ms at 400 kHz,
  under 0.5 ms at 100 kHz" was wrong and is corrected.
- **Also corrected.** The notes said health names tick time while a die is in it. It does not: a die in tick time
  with nothing else wrong is `ok`, and the record's mode byte is where a reader sees it.
- **Not added.** The driver does not print its count of host-timed and rejected brackets or the bounds' width.
  Health text is fixed text, and a driver's figures are shown only with a reason, never for a device that is `ok`
  (`engine/core`, `Lifecycle::figures`), which a die in tick time is. Showing them would change a shared interface
  and what every consumer reads for a healthy device. The tool's rows above give the same answer from the capture.
- **Untested on board.** The fix is checked on the simulated chip only. The next board run is its board check: the
  accelerometer's from-bind capture should switch at about batch 14 to 16 (`b8.mode_changes`), and
  `b8.tick.grid_step_ms` should no longer be near zero. If it switches late again, the rows say which condition
  held it.

Parked, not followed up (not a failure of a listed check): the gyroscope's from-bind tick-time segment has gaps up
to 8.2 ms between drains (20 of them), which is its spread of the drains and is shown, not judged. The first board
run's +668 ms span (B8-REPLAY above) has a likely cause now, not shown: that capture began 3 s after bind with no
reader before it, so its first read could return a full ring, up to 0.73 s of records older than the window. Its
file has stamps only, so the backlog cannot be counted from it.
