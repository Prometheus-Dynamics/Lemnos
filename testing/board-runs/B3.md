# B3: Linux GPIO, PWM and UART

Status: untested on board. Request building, attribute order and error paths are covered by unit
tests. The UART was exercised over a pseudo-terminal, but no real line, channel or port has been
driven.

## GPIO

On the CM5, with a free line on `gpiochip0` wired to something observable:

1. Claim it as `gpiochip0:N` with a driver, and drive it high and low.
   Pass: the level follows.
2. Claim a line another process holds.
   Pass: the device reports `BUSY` and retries.
3. Configure an input with edges on both directions and pulse it.
   Pass: one edge per transition, in order, with increasing `seq`.

## PWM

On a channel with a scope or fan attached:

1. Claim it as `pwmchipN:C` while unexported, and apply a period and duty.
   Pass: the waveform matches, and the channel is unexported on release.
2. Move to a shorter period, then a longer one, while it's running.
   Pass: neither change is refused by the kernel.

## UART

With a loopback or a known peer on a real tty:

1. Claim it by name, set a standard rate, and send and receive.
   Pass: bytes match at 115200 and 1000000.
2. Unplug a USB serial adapter while reading.
   Pass: the device reports the failure instead of reading nothing forever.
