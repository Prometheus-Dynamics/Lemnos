# D6 board run: the PiSP back end

Run on the board 2026-11-11 through `lemnosd` (D15, D17): check 2 passed, and the round trip
measured end to end; checks 3 to 8 were not run as written.

Run in a local session after this PR merges, on the CM5 (Raze #2), kernel 7.2.9 from tarball
53679a5788f7912272b412fd78029bccae6f2b1a. Take `/run/lock/board-gpu.lock`. Module loads, unbinds and
rebinds are allowed; report each. Build on NVMe or SSD, never on the Github HDD. Report CM5 timings
only.

A hung back end can need a reboot: the block has no reset the driver can use. Have the board
reachable for a power cycle before running this, and expect the wedge check to end the session on
the block until one.

## Getting the block

`lemnos.ko` built for the board's kernel (`make KDIR=<the image's kernel build> ARCH=arm64
CROSS_COMPILE=...`), then:

```sh
D=1000880000.pisp_be  # confirm under /sys/bus/platform/devices
echo $D > /sys/bus/platform/drivers/pispbe/unbind
insmod lemnos.ko owned=raspberrypi,pispbe pisp_be=raspberrypi,pispbe
echo lemnos > /sys/bus/platform/devices/$D/driver_override
echo $D > /sys/bus/platform/drivers/lemnos/bind
```

Confirm the compatible first (`/proc/device-tree/axi/pisp_be@880000/compatible`); the module refuses a block whose
version register isn't the expected 0x02252700 family. Afterwards, restore: `echo > driver_override`,
rebind the stock driver, and confirm its nodes come back. Nothing else may hold the block.

The camera proof tool drives this end to end; before it exists, drive the stream directly: a small
program opens `/dev/lemnos`, makes the stream with `LEMNOS_IOC_PISP_BE_CREATE` on CMA heap dma-bufs,
registers the input with `LEMNOS_STREAM_IOC_REGISTER`, and posts a job. `lemnos_pisp_be_check` shows
the shape of it, and `drivers/pisp/tests/kernel_fixture.rs` builds a real configuration.

## Checks

1. **Round trip against the stock figure.** The number the design rests on: 505 µs stock, 511 µs on
   dev. Same input as those measurements: 1280x800 16-bit Bayer to NV12, 3 tiles, an idle board. For
   1000 jobs measure from posting the job (just before the `KICK`) to the frame on the ring, on
   both this module and the stock driver (restore it for its half). Report the median and the 99th
   percentile of each. Pass: this module's median within 5% of the stock driver's measured in the same
   run; report the figures whichever way it goes.

2. **The result is right.** An input with a known pattern (colour bars in Bayer) through the same
   configuration. Compare both planes of the output with the stock driver's output for the same
   input and configuration. Pass: identical.

3. **Sustained.** 120 jobs/s for 60 s with two queued. Pass: every job finishes, `refused` stays 0,
   `wedged` stays 0, the frames' identities come back in order.

4. **A refused job never reaches the block.** Post a job whose output address is 4 KiB past its
   buffer. Pass: `KICK` returns `EFAULT`, the block's `BATCH` counters don't move, and the next stream
   runs normally.

5. **Close and unbind with jobs on the block.** Post two jobs and close immediately, 100 times; then
   the same with unbind and rebind. Pass: every buffer's dma-buf reference returns to what the test
   holds (`count` in `/proc/<pid>/fdinfo/<fd>`), no IOMMU fault in `dmesg`, no wedge.

6. **Does the block obey what the module proved?** The module proves the bytes a tile may touch from
   its documented sizes. The IOMMU is the hardware check on that: run checks 1 to 3 with the block
   behind the IOMMU (it is, on this board) and look for faults. An IOMMU fault on memory the job
   was entitled to would mean the extents are too small; one on memory it wasn't would be a hole.

7. **The counters are as the driver assumes.** While running, log `BATCH` and the job accounting's
   view for the first 100 jobs. Pass: started and done move as `lpb_process` expects, with no
   `wedged`. A wedge here means the accounting assumption is wrong, and the block will need a
   reboot.

8. **A real wedge, last.** Only if a way to hang the block is known without risking the board's
   other work (a config the stock driver's sanitiser exists to prevent: TDN enabled with no input
   and no reset, built by hand, which the module refuses, so this needs a test build with that one
   check removed). Pass: dmesg reports the block stopped, the stream ends with `ETIMEDOUT`, buffers
   stay held, `lemnos.ko` refuses to unload. Reboot afterwards.

## Result, 2026-11-11

Run 2026-11-11 on the CM5 (Raspberry Pi Compute Module 5 Rev 1.0, Raze #2), kernel `7.2.9-v8-16k`,
with `lemnos.ko`, `lemnosd` and the tools from PR F2a, built on NVMe and copied to
`/tmp/lemnos-run/` on the board. Raw logs are kept off the repository.

The camera path was taken from the product: its camera service stopped, `rp1-cfe`, the
sensor's kernel driver, the sensor's fixed regulator (`cam0_reg`, RP1 GPIO34) and `pispbe` unbound,
`insmod lemnos.ko owned=raspberrypi,rp1-cfe,raspberrypi,pispbe pisp_be=raspberrypi,pispbe`, the
receiver bound to `lemnos-rp1-csi2` and the back end to `lemnos` through `driver_override`.
`lemnosd` ran a board file with `gpio-power-switch` on `gpiochip15:34` (default on), `ov9782` on
`i2c-10` and `rp1-csi2` on `1f00110000.csi`, 8 buffers of 1280000 bytes, `memory = "dma-buf"`,
`heap = "linux,cma"`. The client was `lemnos-camera-proof` over the daemon's socket. Afterwards
everything was put back and the product's camera produced its 478 receiver interrupts a second
again, as before the run. The back end was the `pisp-be` driver with `config` 2 × 16720 bytes
(8 job slots), `input` 2 × 2048000 and `output0` 4 × 1600000, all dma-buf from CMA; the client was
`lemnos-isp-proof` (`testing/crates/camera-proof/src/bin/lemnos-isp-proof.rs`).

1. Not the raw node's figure: post to frame through `lemnosd` and the client, one job at a time,
   1000 jobs: median 2426 µs, p99 2477 µs, max 2534 µs. With two jobs queued the block finished
   1000 jobs in 0.842 s (1188 jobs/s, 0.84 ms each), so most of the single-job time is the daemon's
   and the client's, not the block's. The stock driver's 505 µs was not measured on this board.
2. A colour-bar frame from the receiver, unpacked to 16-bit Bayer, through a Bayer-to-NV12
   configuration from the configuration library: the luma of each eighth of the middle line is
   246, 217, 169, 140, 105, 76, 29, 0 (the bars, in order), 800 of 800 lines equal the middle one,
   and the Cb of each eighth is 133, 5, 176, 48, 212, 84, 255, 128. Pass.
3. 2000 jobs, 0 errors, 0 dropped; not the 60 s at 120 jobs/s written here.
4 to 8. Not run.

## Result, 2026-11-11, second run (F2b)

Same board and setup, with `lemnos.ko`, `lemnosd` and the tools from PR F2b. The raw figures come
from `lemnos-be-raw` (`testing/crates/camera-proof/src/bin/lemnos-be-raw.rs`), which drives the
module's back-end node through the driver's `be_hw::LinuxHardware` with no daemon, or the stock
`pispbe` driver's `pispbe-config`, `pispbe-input` and `pispbe-output0` nodes with dma-buf
buffers from CMA, on the same job: the colour-bar frame from the receiver, unpacked to 16-bit
Bayer at stride 2560, to NV12 at stride 1280, 3 tiles, built with the configuration library.
Each side busy-polls for the end, one job at a time; the stock half times from queueing the
output buffer (the last of the three) to dequeueing it, the module's from posting the job to the
frame on the ring. `pispbe` was bound alone for its half and unbound for the module's.

1. 1000 jobs each, on an idle board. Stock `pispbe`: median 738 µs, p99 756 µs, max 845 µs.
   This module: median 739 µs, p99 776 µs, max 866 µs. Within 5 %: pass. The 505 µs in the
   design was not reproduced on this board by either driver. **Failure found in the tool, not the
   driver:** the stock driver registers two groups of nodes with the same names, and the first
   run took nodes from both by directory order, so no job ever formed (`no output in 2 s`); the
   tool now takes the lowest-numbered node of each name.
2. Both outputs hash `651730e8e82d7885` over all 1536000 bytes (Y and CbCr): identical. Luma by
   eighths 246, 217, 169, 140, 105, 76, 29, 0 on both.
3. Through `lemnosd` and `lemnos-isp-proof --rate 120 --seconds 60`: 7200 of 7200 jobs with two
   queued in 60.0 s, identities in order, `dropped +0`, `errors +0`. The single-job and two-queued
   figures again: median 2395 µs, p99 2480 µs; 1231.8 jobs/s.
4. A job whose output address is 4 KiB past its output buffer: the post fails (`IO`; the
   module's `EFAULT` maps to it) and the stream is over, as the module ends a stream on a refused
   job. A new stream then ran 10 of 10 jobs, and its output is the same. The block's `BATCH`
   counters are not exposed and were not read.
5. 100 streams closed with two jobs posted on the block: after each, a new stream ran 10 of 10
   jobs. 100 unbinds of the back end from `lemnos` with a client posting jobs, each followed by a
   bind: 100 of 100 bound again (the probe each time and nothing else in `dmesg`), and a client
   afterwards ran 1000 jobs with the same median (739 µs) and output hash. The client of an
   unbound block sees no frame and stops on its own 2 s limit. The dma-buf counts in `fdinfo` were
   not read.
6. No IOMMU fault in `dmesg` after checks 1 to 5 and the 7200 jobs of check 3.
7. Not run: the module's `BATCH` view and job accounting are not exposed to user space.
8. Not run: it needs a test build of the module with a check removed and a reboot afterwards.
